## Bottom line

Mexico is likely to keep broad access to the U.S. market and working security cooperation with Washington through 2027, but under chronic uncertainty rather than a clean settlement. Both sides depend on each other too much for a rupture: roughly 85% of Mexican exports to the U.S. still enter tariff-free, and Mexico has delivered visible security results and prisoner transfers that give Washington something to point to. The main risk is not a collapse of the trade deal but a slow squeeze, in which annual USMCA reviews and U.S. security demands keep investment decisions on hold and growth near 1.5%.

## Key drivers

- **Trade dependence.** The U.S. declined to renew USMCA on July 1, 2026, triggering annual reviews, though the agreement stays in force until 2036 and the U.S. has raised about 60 issues with Mexico.
- **Security bargaining.** Washington pairs pressure (threats of unilateral action) with demands for extraditions and arrests, while President Sheinbaum offers cooperation and rejects interference.
- **Weak but stable economy.** Banxico expects about 1.5% growth in 2026, inflation is near 4%, and the central bank cut its rate to 6.25% in November.

## What to watch

- The next USMCA review steps, including the January 12, 2027 deadline for written comments to the U.S. Trade Representative.
- Any U.S. unilateral military action or new conditions on security cooperation. This would be the clearest sign the assessment is wrong.
- Monthly homicide data and later INEGI revisions, since official figures are often revised upward and political killings continue.
- How the results of the November 3 U.S. midterms change trade and security policy toward Mexico.
- Banxico decisions, the peso, and third-quarter GDP.
